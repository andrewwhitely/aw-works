I recently took a trip to Mexico City and wanted to make a recap of my experience there. Not a formal guide, must-dos, or anything like that. Just some things I think are worth the time if you're in the city.

Quick notes: I didn't get to do everything I wanted to, so this isn't a comprehensive list of all the things to do in the city. Just some of the things I was able to do and would recommend. Mexico City Airport is/was also under construction/renovation so be prepared for noise and dust depending on which terminal or gate you're at. Lastly, the drive was about an hour from the airport or city. I thought it may have glitched but traffic can be very bad, so just be prepared for that and plan accordingly. However Ubers were plentiful and affordable, so getting around the city was easy. An Uber 'Comfort' was big enough to fit 2 people and 2 standard carry-on sized suitcases, but if you have larger bags or more people you'll likely need an Uber Black or to multiple rides.

## Food & Drinks

Obviously the food is amazing. Easily some of the best tacos that I've had, and outside of that some of the greatest dining experiences I've been able to enjoy as well. This list isn't in any particular order, but here are some of the places I went to and would recommend:

### **Masala y Maíz** <MichelinStar />

    Chefs Norma Listman and Saqib Keval blend Indian, Mexican, and African cuisines into an amazing dining experience. Share the food family style and explore all the options. Drinks are delicious, and the service is fantastic. This is _actually_ a must-do if you're able to get a reservation. The Michelin star is beyond well deserved.

### **Handshake Speakeasy**

    As of writing, this is rated the No. 2 bar in the world on the World's 50 Best Bars List. Although it's named a speakeasy, there's no need to try and find a hidden entrance and say a secret password. You'll get brought in by a member of their team, brought to your table, and given a rundown of the menu. The cocktails are amazing, the food is delicious, although they are snack-sized so don't expect to leave stuffed. While it isn't a must-do, if you like well crafted cocktails this is certainly something to try and check out. Definitely get a reservation, walk-ups are available but no guarantee you'll get seated.

### **Tacos del Valle (Roma Norte)** <BibGourmand />

    I had probably my favorite tacos here. A "small" menu, but super filling. If you go and find that there's a long line, just go ahead and ask for a menu and do a takeout order instead. We waited for about 45 minutes before we realized that was an option and would have definitely done that sooner.

### **Taquería Orinoco**

    Another great taco spot. There are a couple locations but I assume they are all good. Extremely fast, great sauces, delicious tacos. My second favorite tacos of the trip, but my No. 1 al pastor taco.

### Panadería Rosetta

    A great bakery with a huge menu of pastries. The line can be long but it moves quick, especially if you're taking it to go. The caramel tres leches was delicious, and the seasonal flavors are incredible.

### ODETTE

    Another amazing bakery, sort of a long line but the pastries were top tier.

### Coffee Shops

    One thing we found was that a lot of the coffe shops don't use syrups. If you're looking for a sweet drink, you may be out of luck. But there are still plenty of options for espresso, pour overs, lattes, and more. Some that we went to are:

    - **Qūentin Café**
    - **Blend Station**
    - **Nice Day Panaderia** (also has great breakfast and pastries)
    - **CUMBÉ Coffee Roasters**
    - **Café Nin** (also a sister restaurant to Panadería Rosetta, so you can get their pasteries here if you don't want to wait there)

## Museums

- ### Frida Kahlo Museum

    If you enjoy art in any way you surely know of Frida Khalo. Her home is a museum that goes over the history of her life and art. It's a beautiful home and did an incredible job of showing how her life and art were intertwined. I learned a lot about her life especially the disablities, surgeries, and how she was able to take all of the trauma she went through and how it fueled her to create such beautiful work. Tickets here also get you access to the Diego Rivera Museum, which is a museum dedicated to her husband and his work, about 20 minutes away. We didn't end up going here but if you're a fan of his work it's worth checking out.

## Experiences

### **Hot Air Balloon Ride & Pyramid Tour**

    A super common thing people book when visiting is a hot air balloon ride over the pyramids. Personally I'm afraid of heights and was a little terrified of this, but it was an awesome experienc and would recommend it. Definitely book transportation as a part of your package, since the pyramids and balloons are about an hour away from the city. The balloon ride itself was great - the views were incredible and it wasn't scary at all. I didn't even realize we were in the air until I looked around and we were above some trees. After the ride you get dropped off and are given some breakfast and have the option to tour the pyramids or head back to the city. We didn't tour around the pyramids, but if you do there's the option to also buy tickets to get a guided tour of them. I'm not sure if you can climb or go inside of them.

## Neighborhoods

    We stayed in Reforma, which wasn't bad but based on everything we ended up doing we would probably not stay in again. It was about a 20 minute walk to anywhere we wanted to go. However our hotel was great and there was still a ton of restaurants and bars nearby.

    Some great options to stay in are:
    - **Roma Norte/Roma Sur**
    - **Condesa**
    - **Juárez**
    - **Polanco**
    - **Reforma**
    - **Centro**

All in all, this was an amazing trip and I absolutely will be going back to Mexico City. I barely scratched the surface of all the things to do, see, eat, and experience there. We only spent ~4 days there, and I'd recommend this for anything from a weekend trip to an extended stay.
